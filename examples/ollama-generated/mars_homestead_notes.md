# Notes from a Mars Homestead

## Chapter 1: Red Dust Mornings

### The Greenhouse Bell

Every morning on Sol 184, the greenhouse bell rang before sunrise. It was not a real bell, only a soft tone routed through the habitat speakers, but everyone treated it as a promise. The basil trays needed misting, the potato roots needed inspection, and the algae tanks needed another patient calibration. Leena liked the work because plants gave honest feedback. If the colony made a mistake, leaves curled before systems failed. In a place where every wall separated breath from vacuum, that kind of warning felt generous.

### A Repair Shared

When the outer airlock sensor froze, nobody asked whose job it was. Malik brought the toolkit, Sera brought the diagnostic slate, and Leena brought the heat packs meant for seed crates. The repair took three hours and six jokes, most of them terrible. By the time pressure readings stabilized, the storm had painted every window orange. They stood together in the corridor, tired and relieved, knowing the homestead survived less because of heroic speeches than because people kept showing up with what they had.
