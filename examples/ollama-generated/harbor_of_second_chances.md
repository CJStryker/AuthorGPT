# Chapter 1. After the Waterline

## 1.1 The Mark on the Door

The flood left a brown line across every shop door on Pelican Street. Nora traced the mark on her bakery with two fingers and understood that repairs would require more than plywood. Customers arrived with mops, borrowed fans, and stories of what they had saved first. The town had always measured people by what they owned, but the storm changed the arithmetic. That morning, usefulness mattered more than pride, and Nora began by unlocking the door for anyone willing to help.

## 1.2 Bread for Volunteers

By noon the ovens worked again, though the display cases were still fogged with salt. Nora baked plain loaves because plain loaves could feed crews, neighbors, and strangers without ceremony. Her brother Daniel appeared at the back entrance after five silent years, carrying flour as if it were an apology. She did not forgive him immediately. She did, however, point him toward the mixer. Some reconciliations begin not with speeches but with shared work and the smell of bread returning to a ruined street.
