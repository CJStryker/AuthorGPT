# Chapter 1. The First Sprout

## 1.1 Soil Made from Dust

The first lunar tomato sprout looked too delicate for history. It leaned under grow lights in a tray of engineered soil, surrounded by sensors that measured moisture, mineral balance, and every nervous breath from the crew. Commander Imani refused to call it a miracle. Miracles could not be repeated, and the settlement needed repeatable methods. Still, when the two green leaves opened, everyone in Habitat Three lowered their voices. The Moon had received its first small argument against barrenness.

## 1.2 Water Discipline

Water discipline shaped every hour of the greenhouse. Condensation was collected from vents, filtered through ceramic membranes, and returned to roots drop by drop. New technicians often thought scarcity made people selfish, but Imani saw the opposite. Scarcity made care visible. A careless valve could cost a harvest, while a patient inspection could feed twenty people. The crew learned to treat maintenance as a civic ritual, a reminder that survival was not one person’s achievement but a pattern held together by many hands.
