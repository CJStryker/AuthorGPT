# Quiet City Friendships

## Chapter 1: The First Apartment

### A Key and a Map

William arrived with two suitcases, a careful budget, and a printed map folded into squares. The apartment was smaller than the photos, but it was quiet, clean, and his. He spent the first evening labeling shelves, counting groceries, and deciding which bus route would cost the least. When the neighbor across the hall knocked to introduce herself, William rehearsed three answers before opening the door. Mara only smiled, handed him a spare building directory, and said the elevator liked to stop between floors. For the first time that day, the unfamiliar city felt less like a puzzle and more like a place that could be learned.

### Necessary Conversations

The next morning, the laundromat machine swallowed William’s coins and refused to start. He disliked asking strangers for help, not because he disliked people, but because every request felt like a debt. An older man named Mr. Alvarez noticed him reading the faded instructions for the third time and explained the trick: lift the handle, press twice, then wait. William thanked him with exact seriousness. Mr. Alvarez laughed and told him that gratitude was not a contract. William thought about that sentence all afternoon, wondering whether kindness could exist without an invoice attached.
